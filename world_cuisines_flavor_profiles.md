This is the definitive "High-Resolution" map of world cuisines, categorized by shared history, climate-driven ingredients, and flavor philosophies.

1. The European Clusters
Mediterranean: Built on the "Holy Trinity" (olive oil, wheat, wine). Fresh, herb-forward, and vegetable-heavy.

Examples: Italy, Greece, Southern France.

Balkan: The crossroads of Ottoman and Slavic influences. Smoky, meat-centric, featuring phyllo pastries and salty cheeses.

Examples: Bosnia, Serbia, Bulgaria, Albania.

Nordic (Scandinavian): Focused on preservation. Clean flavors, root vegetables, cold-water fish, and rye.

Examples: Sweden, Norway, Denmark.

Central European & Germanic: Hearty and comforting. Schnitzels, dumplings, sausages, and fermented cabbage (sauerkraut).

Examples: Germany, Austria, Czechia, Poland.

Western European (Atlantic): Butter-based cooking, refined techniques, and "meat and two veg" traditions.

Examples: France (Northern), UK, Ireland, Belgium.

2. The African Clusters
Maghreb (North Africa): Fragrant and floral. Tagines, couscous, and the use of dried fruits with savory spices.

Examples: Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia.

West African: Defined by "Spice and Starch." High-heat peppers, palm oil, and thick, umami-rich soups served with "swallows" (fufu).

Examples: Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Ivory Coast.

East African & The Horn: Grain-heavy with unique spice blends like berbere. Characterized by Injera (sourdough flatbread) and lentil stews.

Examples: Ethiopia, Eritrea, Somalia.

Swahili Coast (East African Maritime): A tropical blend of African, Indian, and Arabic flavors—coconut milk, cloves, and ginger.

Examples: Kenya, Tanzania, Zanzibar.

Central African: Earthy rainforest cuisine. Heavy use of cassava leaves, peanuts, and plantains.

Examples: Congo DR, Cameroon.

Southern African: The "Rainbow Cuisine." A mix of indigenous Bantu traditions, Dutch influence, and Indian/Malay spices.

Examples: South Africa, Namibia.

3. The Asian Clusters
East Asian: The rice and fermentation belt. Soy sauce, miso, and ginger are key. Prioritizes balance (Sweet/Sour/Salty/Bitter/Umami).

Examples: China, Japan, Korea.

South Asian: The spice powerhouse. Complex layering of dry spices, ghee, and pulses (lentils/beans).

Examples: India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal.

Southeast Asian (Mainland): Fresh, punchy, and aromatic. Heavy use of fish sauce, lemongrass, galangal, and lime.

Examples: Thailand, Vietnam, Cambodia.

Maritime Southeast Asian: Nutty and coconut-heavy. Satays, peanut sauces, and rich curries.

Examples: Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines.

Central Asian: The Silk Road palette. Lamb-centric, massive rice pilafs (Plov), and hand-pulled noodles.

Examples: Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Afghanistan.

4. The Middle Eastern Clusters
Levantine: The gold standard of "Meze." Hummus, falafel, zesty salads, and grilled kebabs.

Examples: Lebanon, Jordan, Syria, Israel/Palestine.

Persian: Sophisticated and floral. Saffron, pomegranate, and slow-cooked stews with nuts and fruits.

Examples: Iran.

Gulf (Khaleeji): Spiced rice dishes (Machboos) and dates, influenced heavily by ancient spice trade routes.

Examples: Saudi Arabia, Qatar, UAE.

5. The Americas & Caribbean
Mesoamerican: The "Three Sisters" (Corn, Beans, Squash) plus a massive variety of chilies and chocolate.

Examples: Mexico, Guatemala.

Andean: High-altitude soul food. Dozens of potato varieties, quinoa, and corn.

Examples: Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador.

Southern Cone (South American Atlantic): Large-scale cattle ranching culture. Focused on open-fire grilling (Asado) and European-style pastries.

Examples: Argentina, Uruguay, Southern Brazil.

Tropical Latin / Caribbean: A vibrant mix of Spanish, African, and Indigenous roots. Plantains, rice and beans, and jerk/creole seasoning.

Examples: Cuba, Puerto Rico, Haiti, Jamaica, Coastal Colombia.

6. The Fusion Front (The Anglosphere)
Modern Settler Cuisines: These lack a single ancient tradition but act as global "Remix Centers." They take elements from all the above to create high-concept fusion or iconic "New World" staples (like the Hamburger or the Flat White).

Examples: USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand.
