# Recipe Description Taxonomy — Design Spec
**Date:** 2026-05-20  
**Project:** What's Cooking  
**Status:** Approved

---

## Context

The `description` field on recipes is rendered in italic below the recipe title in the detail view. Most descriptions are either scraped (variable quality) or generated by a lightweight Ollama model with a minimal prompt. The goal is a strict taxonomic ruleset that Gemini (or any LLM) can follow to produce consistently high-quality, two-tier editorial descriptions from title + 4 supporting fields — with zero AI slop.

---

## Input Fields (per recipe)

| Field | Example | Usage rule |
|---|---|---|
| `title` | "Crispy Pork Belly Ramen" | Primary source — never contradict |
| `cuisine` | "Japanese" | Cultural context in body sentence 2 only |
| `main_ingredients` | ["pork belly", "ramen noodles", "soft egg"] | Sensory precision only — never list them |
| `cook_method` | "slow-braised, pan-crisped" | Infer texture result; never name the method itself |
| `occasion` | "weekend" | One-word placement anchor in body sentence 2 |

**Inference latitude: Conservative.**  
Only claim what the title + inputs make undeniable. Do not invent specific garnishes, cheeses, spices, or techniques not implied by the title or ingredient list.

---

## Output Structure

### Tier 1 — Hook (mandatory)
- **Length:** 1 sentence, ≤ 18 words
- **Function:** The single most distinctive sensory or cultural fact the inputs make undeniable
- **Rules:**
  - Leads with flavour, texture, or cultural identity — never with occasion or method
  - Complete declarative sentence (no fragments, no rhetorical questions)
  - Forbidden openers: "This dish", "A delicious", "Perfect for", "Here is", "Introducing"

### Tier 2 — Body (mandatory)
- **Length:** 2–3 sentences, 50–75 words total
- **Sentence structure:**
  1. **Texture/flavour contrast** — the dominant eating experience inferred conservatively from ingredients + method result (not method name). State what you taste or feel, not what you did.
  2. **Cultural/occasion anchor** — where and when this belongs on the table; use `cuisine` and `occasion` here and only here.
  3. **Payoff detail** — one concrete finish, contrast, or structural element that closes with specificity. Must be inferable from inputs; no invention.

---

## Anti-Slop Rules (hard prohibitions)

### Forbidden phrases
```
perfect for          bursting with         elevated
next level           packed with flavour   simple yet
a crowd-pleaser      takes it to the next level
just like grandma's  you won't believe     comfort food at its finest
hearty and satisfying  bold and flavourful  delightfully
weeknight hero       effortless            incredibly
melt-in-your-mouth (except when braised meat genuinely does)
```

### Forbidden structures
- No first-person ("I", "we", "you'll love")
- No imperative hooks ("Try this…", "Make this tonight…")
- No hedging ("can be", "might be", "is often considered")
- No invented specifics — if title says "Pasta Bake" and method is "baked", don't invent the cheese
- No ingredient lists ("featuring chicken, garlic, lemon, and herbs")
- No em-dash abuse (max 1 per description)
- No parenthetical asides

---

## Field Usage Rules (strict)

| Field | Allowed uses | Forbidden uses |
|---|---|---|
| `cuisine` | Body sentence 2 cultural context | Hook; contradicting the title |
| `main_ingredients` | Sensory precision in hook or body (1 ingredient max per sentence) | Listing multiple; naming all of them |
| `cook_method` | Infer texture/result (e.g. braised → yielding; roasted → crisp exterior) | Naming the method ("slow-braised until…", "pan-fried to…") |
| `occasion` | Body sentence 2 placement anchor (one word max) | Hook; repeating in body sentence 3 |

---

## Quality Rubric (self-check before output)

The LLM must verify each output against these gates before returning:

1. **Hook ≤ 18 words?** Count them.
2. **Body 50–75 words?** Count them.
3. **No forbidden phrase present?** Scan the list.
4. **No invented specifics?** Every claim traceable to title or inputs.
5. **No ingredient listing?** Ingredients appear for sensory value, not enumeration.
6. **Hook leads with sensory/cultural identity, not occasion or method?**
7. **Body sentence 3 closes with a concrete payoff, not a vague positive?**

---

## Output Format

```json
{
  "hook": "Single sentence ≤ 18 words.",
  "body": "Sentence one: texture/flavour contrast. Sentence two: cultural and occasion anchor. Sentence three: concrete payoff detail."
}
```

The two fields are stored concatenated with a newline in the existing `description` text column:  
`{hook}\n{body}`

No schema change required.

## Examples

### Input
```
title: "Smoky Black Bean Soup"
cuisine: "Mexican-inspired"
main_ingredients: ["black beans", "chipotle", "smoked paprika"]
cook_method: "simmered"
occasion: "weeknight"
```

### Output
```json
{
  "hook": "Dark, smoky, and deeply savoury — black beans carried by chipotle heat.",
  "body": "The long simmer coaxes the beans into a thick, velvety broth with a slow-building warmth that sits at the back of the palate. A weeknight staple in Mexican home cooking, it asks for nothing more than a wedge of lime and a warm tortilla. Smoked paprika threads through every spoonful, giving the soup a campfire depth that canned beans alone never reach."
}
```

### Input
```
title: "Saffron Seafood Risotto"
cuisine: "Italian"
main_ingredients: ["clams", "mussels", "prawns", "saffron", "arborio rice"]
cook_method: "stirred/simmered"
occasion: "dinner party"
```

### Output
```json
{
  "hook": "Golden, briny, and fragrant — the sea in a bowl.",
  "body": "Each grain of rice absorbs the saffron-stained broth until just yielding, carrying the mineral sweetness of clams and mussels in every bite. This is coastal Italian cooking reserved for dinner parties, where the slow ritual of stirring becomes part of the occasion. Prawns finish at the very end, curling pink into the risotto and holding their snap against the soft grain."
}
```

---

## Storage

- **Column:** `recipes.description` (existing `text`, nullable)
- **Format:** `{hook}\n{body}` as plain text
- **UI rendering:** The existing detail page already renders description in italic `<p>` below the title — no UI change needed for MVP. A future pass can split on `\n` to style hook and body differently.
